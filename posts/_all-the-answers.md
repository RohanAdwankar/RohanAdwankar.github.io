# I hate having all the answers

When I was a kid nobody around me had the answers.

If I wanted to know why something worked I had to go find out. That meant taking it apart. It meant trying things that made no sense. It meant being wrong for a long time before being right.

I did not think of this as a skill. It was just what you did when nobody could tell you.

Looking back, the not knowing was the whole thing. Most of what I can do today came from a long stretch of not having an answer and poking at the problem anyway. The detours were where the capability came from. Nobody plans a detour. You end up on one because you did not know the road.

## The beginner's luck effect

There is a reason beginners sometimes pull off things experts say cannot be done.

The beginner does not know it cannot be done. So they try it. Most of the time it fails. Sometimes it works, and now there is a new thing in the world that the expert ruled out years ago.

This is not luck. It is the absence of a confident answer. The expert has one. The beginner does not. The beginner gets to search.

I got to be a beginner for a long time. Nobody handed me a verdict, so I kept searching well past the point where a verdict would have stopped me.

## Now there is always an answer

Today I can ask a model anything and get an answer back in seconds.

The answer is fluent. It is complete. It is delivered with total confidence.

It is also wrong a lot of the time. Not obviously wrong. Wrong in the way a confident expert is wrong, where the reasoning sounds fine and the conclusion is off.

Here is the part that bothers me. Even when the answer is wrong, it ends the search.

You asked. Something answered. The question feels closed. You move on to the next thing. There is no long stretch of not knowing anymore. There is no reason to take the thing apart yourself, because you were told how it works.

And if the answer said something cannot be done, you believe it. That is the expert's verdict, delivered at zero cost, to every beginner, on every question, before they ever try.

## What gets lost

The capability I care about was never the answer. It was what happened while I did not have one.

A model that answers everything with full confidence removes that window. It turns every beginner into someone who already knows what is impossible. It undoes beginner's luck at scale.

The model is not lying on purpose. It is doing exactly what it was built to do. The problem is on my side. A confident answer is very hard to ignore, even when I know it might be wrong.

## What I do about it

I try to keep the window open on purpose.

For things I actually want to get good at, I do not ask first. I try, I fail, I sit in the not knowing for a while. Then I ask, and I treat the answer as one more thing to check rather than the end of the search.

For things I do not care about, I take the answer and move on. That is fine. Not everything needs a detour.

The hard part is telling the two apart before the answer arrives. Once it arrives, it is already too late.

I hate having all the answers. The answers were never the point.
